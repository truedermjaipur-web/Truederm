Mole Removal: When It's Just Cosmetic, and When to See a Dermatologist Sooner

By Dr. Iftekhar Khan, MBBS, MD Dermatology (AIIMS Delhi) · Truederm, C-Scheme, Jaipur
Skin examination at Truederm, C-Scheme, Jaipur

Most people have somewhere between 10 and 40 moles on their body, and the overwhelming majority are completely harmless. But "most are harmless" is different from "yours definitely is" - and the difference matters. Here's how Dr. Khan approaches every mole, skin tag or growth removal request at Truederm.

Cosmetic removal - the common scenario

A lot of removals really are simple and cosmetic: a raised mole that catches on clothing or jewellery, a skin tag in an annoying spot, a cyst that's more nuisance than concern. These are usually quick, low-downtime, in-clinic procedures once Dr. Khan has confirmed there's nothing about the growth that warrants closer investigation first.

The ABCDE rule - what to actually watch for

Before any mole is removed purely for cosmetic reasons, it gets checked against the standard warning signs dermatologists use worldwide:

  • A - Asymmetry: one half doesn't match the other
  • B - Border: edges are irregular, notched or blurred rather than smooth
  • C - Colour: multiple colours or shades within the same mole
  • D - Diameter: larger than about 6mm (roughly the size of a pencil eraser)
  • E - Evolving: any change in size, shape, colour, or new symptoms like itching or bleeding
The single most important sign: change. A mole that has looked the same for years is reassuring. A mole that's different from how it looked six months ago is the one worth getting checked promptly, rather than waiting for your next routine visit.

What happens with a suspicious growth

If a mole or lesion shows any of the signs above, it isn't simply removed and forgotten. Dr. Khan follows standard dermatosurgical protocol - which typically means careful excision and sending the tissue for histopathological (laboratory) examination to confirm exactly what it is, so any next steps are based on an actual diagnosis rather than an assumption.

Other common minor procedures

  • Skin tags - quick removal, minimal to no scarring in most cases
  • Sebaceous cysts - removed to prevent recurrence or infection
  • Xanthelasma - cholesterol deposits around the eyelids, removed carefully given the delicate skin in that area
  • Warts - removed via the method best suited to their location and size

Recovery, in plain terms

Most procedures are done under local anaesthesia as a same-day visit. Stitches, where used, typically come out within 7-10 days, and you'll get specific aftercare instructions to support clean healing and minimise any visible mark.

Have a mole or growth that's changed recently?

Get it examined properly rather than guessing. Dr. Khan will tell you plainly whether it needs attention.
