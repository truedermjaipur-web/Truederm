5 Pigmentation Myths That Are Wasting Your Money

By Dr. Iftekhar Khan, MBBS, MD Dermatology (AIIMS Delhi) · Truederm, C-Scheme, Jaipur
Advanced skin treatment equipment used at Truederm, C-Scheme, Jaipur

Pigmentation is one of the most common concerns patients bring to Truederm — and also one of the most misunderstood. Between social media advice and neighbourhood "remedies," a lot of patients arrive having already tried things that made their skin worse. Here are the myths Dr. Khan corrects most often.

Myth 1: "Lemon juice and home remedies lighten dark spots"

Lemon juice is acidic and can irritate the skin barrier, sometimes triggering more pigmentation rather than less — especially when followed by sun exposure. Natural doesn't mean gentle. Actual pigment-correcting ingredients need to be used at the right concentration under guidance, not applied raw.

Myth 2: "Sunscreen isn't necessary if I'm indoors most of the day"

UV rays aren't the only trigger — visible light from windows and even indoor lighting can worsen melasma in people who are prone to it. Daily sunscreen, reapplied properly, is the single most important step in any pigmentation treatment plan. Skipping it undoes the rest of the routine.

Myth 3: "Pigmentation and melasma are the same thing"

They're related but not identical. General pigmentation can result from acne marks, sun damage or ageing, while melasma is a distinct condition strongly influenced by hormones and tends to be more stubborn and recurrence-prone. Treating melasma like ordinary pigmentation often leads to frustration — it needs a different, more patient approach.

Myth 4: "Stronger creams work faster"

Over-the-counter creams with unregulated concentrations of skin-lightening agents can cause thinning of the skin, rebound pigmentation, or steroid-related side effects with prolonged unsupervised use. Faster isn't always safer — a dermatologist-guided routine paced correctly gets better, longer-lasting results.

Myth 5: "One laser session will clear it completely"

Pigmentation, especially melasma, is typically managed rather than "cured" in a single sitting. A realistic plan usually combines in-clinic procedures like chemical peels or laser therapy with a consistent home skincare routine and sun protection — maintained over months, not days.

What actually works: an accurate diagnosis of the type of pigmentation, a customised topical routine, appropriate in-clinic procedures spaced correctly, and non-negotiable daily sun protection.

What to expect at a consultation

Dr. Khan examines the pattern, depth and likely trigger of your pigmentation before recommending a plan — because a treatment that works beautifully for sun-induced spots can do very little for hormonal melasma, and vice versa.
